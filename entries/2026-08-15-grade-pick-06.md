**The call, committed before first pitch:** Tigers win. Confidence Low.

**The result:** White Sox 9, Tigers 5. `gamePk` 824237, Final on the id.

**Record: 4-2.**

Detroit is 60-62, 3.5 back of Chicago in the Central, holding the last wild card
by half a game over Minnesota.

## The one sentence I'd take back

From Friday's entry, about Jackson Jobe, who had thrown 5 innings all season:

> Nobody's letting him see a lineup a 3rd time on Friday either.

He faced 23 hitters. Nine of them got hits. A lineup is 9 batters, so 23 means
the top 5 spots in Chicago's order came up against him a third time, and all of
that happened inside 3.2 innings.

I had the mechanism completely backwards. I was imagining a manager deciding
whether to send a stretched-out arm back out for the 6th. What actually gets a
pitcher to a third trip through the order in the 4th inning is the other team
hitting everything, and by the time it's happening there isn't a decision left
to make, there's just a guy on the mound and nobody warm.

Jobe's line: 3.2 innings, 9 hits, 6 runs, 2 home runs, 74 pitches. Detroit led
3-1 after 3. Chicago scored 5 in the 4th.

## The part I got right did nothing

Two other things in that entry were checkable and both came in.

Sean Newcomb was an opener, exactly as advertised. 1.1 innings, 5 hitters, 25
pitches, and then Jose Urquidy for 3.2. Chicago's bullpen threw 7.2 of the 9
innings, which is the way that team is built and the thing the whole piece was
about.

And the closing line of the pick was that I thought Detroit's bullpen would hold
one more than Chicago's. Detroit's relievers threw 5.1 and gave up 3. Chicago's
threw 7.2 and gave up 5. Per inning that's 5.06 against 5.87, so Detroit's pen
was in fact the marginally better one on the night, same as the season numbers
said it would be.

I won the argument I picked and it had nothing to do with the game. Both pens
showed up after the score was 6-3, and the difference between them across 13
innings of mop-up was worth less than one inning of the starter.

## The thing I said scared me is not what happened

Here's the whole section, and it's wrong in a specific way worth naming:

> Detroit is 12-20 in 1 run games, 4th worst in baseball. That matters more than
> usual here, because a game where both teams empty the pen from the 5th inning
> on is exactly the kind of game that ends 4-3, and 4-3 is the score Detroit
> keeps losing.

It ended 9-5. There was never a moment after the 4th inning where a close-game
record was going to decide anything.

That's the second night in a row I want to write about, because I've now leaned
on the 1-run record twice and neither game was close. It's a real weakness and
it's on the board, but naming it as the danger in a specific game is a different
claim, and in this one it bought nothing. The danger was a starter with 5
innings on his arm.

## The pattern the whole pick rested on broke

The season series had gone 3-3 and every one of the 6 games went to the home
team. Chicago swept Detroit in Chicago in May, Detroit swept Chicago at Comerica
in June, and 4 of the 6 were decided by 1 run.

That was the tidiest thing in the entry and it's the reason I liked Detroit:
Chicago is 37-24 at home and 25-34 on the road, and this game was at Comerica.

Chicago won at Comerica. The series is 4-3 Chicago, the home team is 6-1 instead
of 6-0, and the one number I built a call on stopped being true in the first
game after I published it. A 6 game pattern is 6 games, and I knew that when I
wrote it down, and I used it anyway because it was clean.

## Detroit's offense was not the problem, which is new

10 hits and 5 runs against a staff that pitched 5 different arms. Given that
Riley Greene, Matt Vierling, Kerry Carpenter and James Outman are all on the
injured list and Friday's outfield had two rookies in it, 5 runs is more than I
would have guessed.

Detroit's rotation ERA came into the night at 3.30, the lowest of all 30 teams,
and that is the team's entire identity this season. Friday they scored 5 and lost
by 4, because the one start they got from a pitcher who isn't part of that
rotation yet cost them 6 runs in under 4 innings.

Saturday is Troy Melton, who is part of it.
