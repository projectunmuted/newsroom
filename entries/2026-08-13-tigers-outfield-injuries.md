Riley Greene went on the 10-day injured list Wednesday with a right hamstring
strain. He's got 492 plate appearances and an .816 OPS, easily Detroit's best
outfielder, and he joins Matt Vierling and Kerry Carpenter, who are already
there. 3 outfielders, on a team that's half a game out of the last wild card
spot with 42 to play.

Worth being accurate about how big a bat this is, because I nearly wasn't. Greene
is not Detroit's best hitter. Among the 6 guys with 300 or more plate
appearances he's 3rd in OPS, behind Dillon Dingler at .844 and Kevin McGonigle at
.819, and McGonigle has more plate appearances than he does. Greene's the best
outfielder on the roster and the 3rd best hitter, and those are different
sentences.

So it reads like the season just ended. I don't think it does, and I want to
show you why, including the part that argues the other way.

## First, they didn't all go down at once

This matters more than it sounds, because "we just lost 3 outfielders" and "we
lost 1 more outfielder having already lost 2" are pretty different sentences.

From the league's own transactions feed:

| Player | Placed | Injury | Eligible to return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kerry Carpenter | Jul 27 | Left foot plantar fasciitis | Aug 6 |
| Matt Vierling | Jul 31, retro to Jul 30 | Left adductor strain | Aug 9 |
| Riley Greene | Aug 12 | Right hamstring strain | Aug 22 |

Carpenter's been out 17 days and Vierling 14. Both of them passed their 10-day
minimum a while back and neither is back, which is its own small worry, and
plantar fasciitis is the kind of thing that lingers. But the point for right now
is that Detroit's already been playing shorthanded for 2 weeks. Only 1 of these
3 is new information.

On Greene specifically, A.J. Hinch said the club thinks the strain is mild, and
that Greene felt it slowing up at home plate and never hobbled, which Hinch
called a good sign as opposed to a big mid-belly grab. A Cody Stavenhagen quote
going around r/motorcitykitties has Hinch saying he wouldn't have been able to
play the rest of this series or the White Sox weekend anyway, which I'm passing
on second hand rather than from the piece itself. Take the 10-day minimum at
face value and that's about 9 games.

Vierling's at .590. That's below the level of a randomly grabbed bench guy, and
I'll show you where that .604 comes from in a second. Carpenter's at .692, which
is under the league's .719 and under Detroit's own team mark of .727. Greene is
the only one of the 3 who was clearly making the team better by being in the
lineup.

The replacement number isn't something I remembered, it's derived. Every
non-pitcher in baseball with under 150 plate appearances this season, 267 of
them, 15,352 trips to the plate, hits .280/.324 for a .604 OPS. That's the
population a club actually reaches for when a regular goes down, and it's
exactly the group Detroit's reaching for now. Julks has 2 plate appearances this
season, Clark has 50, Malgeri 57, Outman 100. All 4 of them are inside that
bucket.

I deliberately did not use Detroit's replacements' own numbers as the baseline,
because Max Clark hitting .804 in 50 plate appearances tells you nothing and
using it would flatter the argument I'm making. The league-wide bucket is the
more conservative choice and it's the one the math below runs on.

## So what's it worth in wins

Fit runs per plate appearance on OPS across all 30 teams this season and you
get runs/PA = -0.1218 + 0.3340 x OPS, r-squared .830. So 100 points of OPS is
worth about .033 runs every time you come up. Detroit's scored 549 and allowed
462 in 120 games, which puts a win at about 10.4 runs.

Give each guy the plate appearances he was taking per game he played, times the
games he misses:

| Scenario | Runs | Wins |
|---|---|---|
| Greene out all 42 remaining | 12.5 | 1.20 |
| Carpenter out all 42 | 4.0 | 0.38 |
| Vierling out all 42 | -0.6 | -0.06 |
| **All 3, rest of season** | **15.8** | **1.52** |
| Greene for the 10-day minimum, about 9 games | 2.7 | 0.26 |

Worst case, all 3 miss every remaining game, it's about a win and a half, and
79 percent of that is Greene. Vierling's line is negative, meaning the numbers
say Detroit is very slightly better with him on the shelf, which I'd treat as
"costs nothing" rather than as an actual gain.

The realistic case is the bottom row. Greene misses 9 or so, the other 2 come
back somewhere in there, and the whole thing costs a quarter of a win.

## Now the argument against all of that

Carpenter. His .692 is by a distance the worst season of his career. He went
.811, .932 and .788 the 3 years before this one, 1,219 plate appearances that
weight out to about .832. Treating a guy's worst 259 plate appearances as his
true talent is exactly the error I'd complain about if somebody else did it.

Run him at .832 instead and he's worth 10.2 runs over 42 games rather than 4.0,
right about a full win on his own. That takes the 3-man total from 1.52 wins to
2.12 and drops Greene's share from 79 percent to 57. So the honest version of
the headline is that 2 of the 3 matter and 1 of them doesn't, not that this is
all about Greene.

Small note on that .832, since I'd want to know: it's his 2023 to 2025 seasons
weighted by plate appearances, which is an approximation. Doing it properly means
rebuilding on-base and slugging from the raw components rather than averaging
3 OPS figures. It'd move the number a little, not enough to change anything here.

Vierling doesn't get the same defence. He was at .717 and .735 in his first 2
Detroit years, then .617 in 100 plate appearances last year, then .590. That's
2 years of going the wrong way, not 1 bad stretch.

A few other things this method can't see, and I'd rather say them than have you
find them:

- **OPS knows nothing about defence.** Vierling's the center fielder. Handing
  center to Outman or a rookie is a real cost and none of the arithmetic above
  can price it. If Vierling's glove is worth half a win out there, most of my
  "he costs nothing" disappears.
- **A team-level fit used on 1 player pretends to be more precise than it is.**
  That r-squared of .830 is across 30 teams and thousands of plate appearances
  each. Pointing it at 176 plate appearances from 1 man gives you a number with
  a decimal point and not really that much behind the decimal point.
- **Greene's rate assumes he plays every game he's available for.** He wouldn't
  have. So 1.20 is a bit high.

## What the last 2 weeks actually looked like

Here's the thing I keep going back and forth on. Since Carpenter went down on
July 27, Detroit's played 14 games and gone 9-5, scoring 7.43 runs a game. Before
that they were at 4.20.

That's a huge number and I don't believe it means much. It's 14 games, and it
includes 14-0 and 13-1 and 11-0 and 11-0 again, so a couple of blowouts are
carrying most of it. Nobody should walk away thinking the Tigers are better
without their outfielders. What it's decent evidence of is the narrower thing:
the offence has not fallen over without Carpenter and Vierling, and it had 14
games to.

## Where this leaves them

59-61, half a game back of Texas for the last spot and level with Minnesota, 42
to go, with 6 teams inside 2 games of each other. It's about as tight as it gets.

And that's the part that keeps me from filing this under "no big deal." A
quarter of a win sounds like nothing, and in a normal August it is nothing. In a
race where 6 teams are separated by 2 games, a quarter of a win is a real slice
of what's left. The injuries aren't a disaster and the panic version of this
story is wrong, but "it's small" and "it doesn't matter" aren't the same claim,
and in this race they come apart.

If Greene's back around the 22nd and Carpenter's foot finally settles down, this
is a footnote. If the hamstring turns into 6 weeks, the number to be scared of
is 1.2 wins, and Detroit doesn't have 1.2 wins to give away.
