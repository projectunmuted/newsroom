**The call: Detroit takes the series finale Thursday afternoon at Comerica.
Confidence: low.**

Yesterday the question here was whether Detroit should be running on Cleveland.
The answer was mostly yes, and the running-game edge in Wednesday night's game
actually belonged to Cleveland, because Framber Valdez has let 13 runners steal
and thrown out none of them.

Thursday afternoon the same series produces the opposite game entirely. Of the
58 qualified starters in baseball, exactly 1 is harder to run on than either of
the 2 men starting this one, and he pitches for Milwaukee.

## Nobody runs on these two

Take every qualified starting pitcher in baseball, 58 of them, and count steal
attempts allowed per 9 innings. Not stolen bases, and not the rate runners get
thrown out, because both of those are mostly a story about the catcher. Attempts
is the pitcher's number. It's his time to the plate, his pickoff move, his
handedness, and it's the closest free measure of whether a runner even considers
it.

Keider Montero is 2nd of 58. Parker Messick is 3rd.

Between them, Messick and Montero have faced 3 attempted steals in 259 innings.
2 median starters over the same workload would face about 20.

And here's the part I liked most: neither of them has thrown out a single
runner. Montero has allowed 1 stolen base and caught nobody. Messick has allowed
2 and caught nobody. This isn't a couple of guys with rocket arms behind them
gunning people down. It's that in 259 innings, 3 people have thought it was
worth trying.

## Which lands almost entirely on Cleveland

Detroit doesn't run. That's been true all year and it was the finding two days
ago: 53 attempts in 1,100 times reached first, 4.8%, dead last of 30 teams. So
Messick shutting down the Detroit running game is like locking a door nobody
uses. Call it 0.4 attempts a game that Detroit wasn't going to make anyway.

Cleveland does run, and does it well. 117 steals, 23 caught, 83.6% success,
attempting on 12.7% of times reached first. In a normal game they reach first
about 9 times, so that's a bit over 1 attempt a game they'd like to have.
Against Montero it's approximately zero.

Now the honest size of that. 1 attempt is not a game. If you handed me a lineup
card and told me only that Cleveland's running game was switched off, I would
move the number by a fraction of a run and no more. What makes it worth writing
about is that Cleveland's offense doesn't have many other tools. They're 28th in
baseball in runs scored, and 26th in OPS against right-handed pitching, which is
what Montero is. The bases were one of the few places they were above average,
and Thursday it's shut.

## The rest of the game

Detroit is 59-60 and 2.5 back of the White Sox with the division wide open. They
have won 8 of 10 and 3 in a row. Cleveland is 58-62, has lost 8 of 10 and 3 in a
row, and is 4 back.

Under the records, the gap is bigger than 1 game in the standings. Detroit has
scored 545 runs and allowed 456, and that run prevention number is 3rd best in
baseball. Cleveland has scored 477 and allowed 506. That's a Detroit team
outscoring its opponents by 89 against a Cleveland team being outscored by 29.

Home team, better team, hotter team, against a lineup that's 26th against
righties. That's the call.

## What scares me

**Messick is the better pitcher and it isn't close.** 2.57 ERA over 23 starts,
1.04 WHIP, 136 strikeouts in 136.2 innings. His fielding independent numbers
back most of it up: 3.19 FIP, 3.44 xFIP. He is a 25-year-old lefty having a
genuinely excellent season and Detroit is exactly league average against
left-handed pitching, 15th of 30 at a .706 OPS. That's the whole case for
Cleveland and it's a good one.

**Montero's numbers are prettier than his pitching.** A 1.01 WHIP looks
outstanding until you notice he has 86 strikeouts in 122.1 innings, which is
about 6.3 per 9, and that his xFIP is 4.57 against a 3.38 ERA. That's a
run-and-a-fifth gap between what he's given up and what his strikeouts, walks
and flyballs say he should have given up. He also gets hit harder at home, 3.79
at Comerica against 2.77 on the road, so the park isn't the trick either. I went
looking for that explanation and it isn't there.

**These 2 have already done this once and Cleveland won.** May 19 at Comerica,
Messick against Montero, Cleveland 4 Detroit 3. Messick went 5 and gave up 2.
Montero went 5, gave up 3, and walked 4 while striking out 1.

**And the season series is 1-6.** Detroit finally beat Cleveland on Monday for
the first time in 7 tries. The piece on this site that went looking for the
reason concluded there mostly isn't one, and I still believe that. But 1-6 is
1-6, and I'm picking the team that owns the losing end of it.
