**Tigers at Pirates, Monday 7:05pm ET at PNC Park. The call: Tigers win.
Confidence High.**

That is the first High I have put on a game, so it's worth saying up front what
it costs. Low means I'm picking a side because picking a side is the job. High
means I like it and I'm going to look stupid if it misses. This one is High.

## The number on the back of the card is an average of two different pitchers

Carmen Mlodzinski's 2026 line is 27 appearances, 99.2 innings, a 3.79 ERA and a
1.40 WHIP. Read straight, that's a solid mid-rotation season and nothing to be
excited about facing.

He hasn't been a starter, though. He's been a swingman, and Pittsburgh has used
him both ways all year, 11 starts and 16 relief outings, interleaved from March
to August. Those two jobs went very differently.

| Role | Outings | IP | ERA | WHIP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starting | 11 | 49.1 | 5.47 | 1.68 |
| Relieving | 16 | 50.1 | 2.15 | 1.13 |

Nearly identical innings, and the ERA more than doubles. A 1.68 WHIP as a
starter means he's putting a man and two thirds on base every inning he starts a
game.

Once. May 8, against a San Francisco club that is 51-72. That's the only time in

The 2 most recent ones are the reason this is a High and not a Low. Since coming
back into the rotation on August 7 he has thrown 5.1 innings across 2 starts and
given up 9 earned runs. Against the Mets: 3 innings, 7 hits, 5 runs. Against
Miami, a club that has scored the 6th fewest runs in the National League: 2.1
innings, 5 hits, 3 walks, 4 runs.

There's no injury behind the gap in his starting log, either. He wasn't on the
injured list at any point this season. Pittsburgh moved him to the bullpen after
May 25, he was good there, and they moved him back. The 2.15 is real. It just
isn't what shows up Monday night.

## Detroit doesn't have to beat him. It has to get past him

If Mlodzinski goes 4, Pittsburgh needs 5 innings from a bullpen carrying a 4.24
ERA over 492.2 innings, which is the most relief work of the 2 clubs in this
series. Detroit's own pen is at 3.78. The comparison isn't close and it's the
part of the game most likely to actually happen, because a starter who has gone
6 once in 11 tries is a starter whose team is planning around the pen already.

Pittsburgh is also short. Ryan O'Hearn, .788 OPS in 398 plate appearances, is on
the 10-day. Oneil Cruz, .822 in 283, is on the 60-day. So is Konnor Griffin.
Endy Rodriguez, .840 in 159, is on the 10-day. That's 4 of their better bats,
and it's the mirror of Detroit's outfield situation rather than an edge to
either side.

## The thing everybody watching this team believes right now, and the numbers say it's wrong

Detroit has lost 4 in a row and 3 straight at home to the White Sox. The fan
reaction to that is that the offense has gone quiet with Riley Greene, Kerry
Carpenter, Matt Vierling and James Outman all hurt.

It hasn't.

Across all 14 games Detroit has played in August the club is scoring **5.64 runs
a game**, against a season rate of 4.56. With 4 outfielders on the shelf and a
lineup running rookies out there, this offense is having its best month of the
year. Brett Callahan tripled in his first big league at bat on Sunday.

What has gone wrong is the run prevention. 9, 4 and 7 allowed in the 3 losses.
Detroit's team ERA is still 3.55, 4th in baseball, so the last week is a slump
rather than a level, and a slump is exactly the thing you want to buy against
somebody with a 5.47 mark in the job he's doing tonight.

## What scares me

**Framber Valdez walks people.** 50 in 133 innings, a 1.39 WHIP, and 5 walks in
5.2 innings his last time out against Cleveland. PNC Park is a hard place to hit
home runs and an easy place to score on singles and traffic, and Valdez's bad
starts are traffic starts. When he's off, he's off early: 0.2 innings and 6 runs
against the Cubs on July 21.

**Detroit is 28-33 on the road** and Pittsburgh is 32-31 at home, so the venue
runs the wrong way.

**Mlodzinski's stuff is not the problem.** 78 strikeouts in 99.2 innings, and his
relief line says that when he only has to face a lineup once, he's genuinely
good. If Pittsburgh has decided to pull him at the 18th batter and hand it to a
fresh arm, the whole shape of this pick changes and the game becomes a bullpen
contest that Detroit's own 27 blown saves make a coin flip.

**And the honest structural one:** Detroit is 60-64 while its run differential
says 71-53. That gap of 11 wins is the largest in baseball. A team that has spent
a season converting good underlying play into losses is a team you should be
slower to back than the numbers suggest, and I'm backing it anyway.

## The call

**Tigers win.** High. Detroit's offense is in its best month of the season, it's
facing a starter who has cleared the 6th inning once since March, and the club
waiting behind him has the worse bullpen of the 2.
