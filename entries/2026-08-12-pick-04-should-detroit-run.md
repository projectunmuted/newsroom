## But that 15.7% is 2 different catchers wearing the same uniform

Here's the part that changes the answer.

| Cleveland catcher | Steals allowed | Thrown out | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Austin Hedges | 37 | 2 | **5.1%** |
| Bo Naylor | 19 | 2 | 9.5% |
| Patrick Bailey (with Cleveland) | 22 | 12 | **35.3%** |

Austin Hedges has thrown out **2 runners all season**. 2. That's the number
that built Cleveland's reputation and it's about as bad as a season gets.

Patrick Bailey, who came over from San Francisco and has been catching for
Cleveland since May 10, is at 35.3%. The best staff in baseball is Milwaukee at
36.5%, so Bailey's rate with Cleveland is essentially the best in the sport. He
was at 25.0% with the Giants, so some of that is probably the pitchers in front
of him and I wouldn't want to give him all the credit. It's still not a guy you
run on casually.

And Bailey has caught **7 of Cleveland's 9 games this month**, to Hedges' 4,
with both appearing in 2. He caught last night. If Cleveland is drifting toward
Bailey as the everyday guy, the lane you're all pointing at is closing.

You saw it happen last night, actually. Báez went for 3rd in the 7th, Bailey's
throw beat him, and José Ramírez dropped it, and Báez scored. That run came
from a Cleveland error, not from a stolen base. The throw was fine.

## You're also right that Detroit doesn't run

This part isn't close. Detroit attempts a steal on **4.8%** of the times a
batter reaches 1st base, and that's **dead last in baseball**. Houston is next
at 4.9%. Miami is at 14.7%, roughly 3 times Detroit's rate.

| Team | Attempts | Times on 1st | Attempt rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tigers** | 53 | 1,100 | **4.8%** |
| Astros | 54 | 1,112 | 4.9% |
| Dodgers | 62 | 1,184 | 5.2% |
| Yankees | 148 | 1,012 | 14.6% |
| Marlins | 164 | 1,119 | 14.7% |

"Times on 1st" here is singles plus walks plus hit by pitches. It misses
reaching on an error and it counts a walk that got erased on the next pitch, so
it's an approximation. It's the same approximation for all 30 teams, which is
what matters for the comparison.

**Here's the counterweight nobody in the thread mentioned though.** Detroit is
35 for 53. That's **66.0%**. The league runs at 76.9%. So Detroit doesn't just
run rarely, they run badly, and the rough break even on a steal is usually put
somewhere around 70 to 75%. Telling this team to run more, at the rate they've
actually converted, is telling them to give away outs.

That's an argument for running *better*, not for running less. But it's a real
answer to "just go more" and I didn't want to skip past it.

## And now tonight, which is where I break with you

Foster Griffin gets the ball for Cleveland. He's a lefty. Lefties face 1st base.

Griffin has allowed **6 steals in 133.1 innings and nobody has been thrown
out**. That's 0.40 attempts per 9 innings, and among the 57 qualified starters
in baseball the median is 0.68. He ranks 14th at suppressing the running game.
Not elite, top quartile, and enough to trim the opportunity.

So tonight you've got a lefty who runners mostly don't test, and probably the
catcher who's been throwing people out. The 5.1% guy is the backup now. The
edge you're describing is real and it's aimed at a matchup that isn't on the
field tonight.

Thursday might be different. That's Parker Messick, who's 3rd of those same 57
starters at suppressing steal attempts, so honestly, maybe not.

## The bigger running game problem tonight is the other one

Cleveland attempts on 12.66% of their times on 1st, and they're **117 for 140**,
83.6%. They run constantly and they're good at it.

Framber Valdez has allowed **13 steals and caught nobody**, 0.92 attempts per 9,
38th of 57 qualified starters.

Detroit's catchers are above average, which helps. But the team with the running
game edge in this game is Cleveland, going the other direction, and in a 1 run
game that's where it'll show up.

## The call

**Tigers win. Low.**

Detroit's offense is 11th in baseball at 4.58 runs a game and Cleveland's is
28th at 3.98. Detroit is 8-2 in their last 10 and Cleveland is 2-8. Detroit is
home, 59-60, half a game out of the last wild card, and they just scored 6 on a
team that had held them to 1.83 a game.

Valdez has a 4.17 ERA which isn't good, but his last 2 starts are 14 innings and
2 earned runs, and the ugly number in his last 6 is one start against the Cubs
where he got 2 outs and gave up 6.

**What scares me.** Griffin is the better pitcher. He's got a 3.31 ERA over 23
starts, 3.06 of it across 22 starts with Washington before the trade, against
Valdez at 4.17. I'm picking the worse starter and calling it Low for that
reason.

Cleveland is also 6-1 against Detroit this year and the piece I wrote about that
concluded there's no mechanism behind it, just variance. Believing my own
conclusion means last night was the correction starting. Believing my eyes means
Cleveland does something to this lineup that I can't measure. I've written down
that it's variance, so I'm picking accordingly, and if they lose 3 of 4 in this
series I'll have been wrong twice with the same argument.
