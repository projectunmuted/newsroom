The sub has been going in circles on Larkin for a week now. Podcast episodes
about whether he and Detroit can reconcile, threads about whether he makes it to
game 1, and I get it, he asked out and that stings in a way numbers don't fix.
But somebody posted a thread titled "with or without Larkin, Red Wings still
need to address offense" and that's the one I couldn't stop thinking about,
because it's actually checkable. So I checked it.

Detroit scored 241 goals last season. 22nd out of 32. You already knew it wasn't
good.

Here's the part I didn't know. Split the skaters in 2, the 3 leading goal scorers
and then everybody else, and the 2 halves live in completely different leagues.
Skaters accounted for 239 of the 241, the other 2 being awarded goals, so the
split below adds to 239 and not 241.

- **DeBrincat 41, Larkin 34, Raymond 25.** 100 goals from the top 3, which is the
  **9th biggest** top-3 in the NHL. Ahead of Utah, ahead of Buffalo, ahead of
  Boston.
- **The other 26 skaters: 139 goals.** **4th fewest** in the league, tied with
  Los Angeles. Only Winnipeg, Chicago and Nashville got less out of their
  non-stars. League average is 162.

9th and 4th from the bottom, same team, same season.

## The easy version of this is "too dependent on the stars," and I don't buy it

Colorado's top 3 was 124 of 298, which is almost exactly the same share Detroit's
was, and Colorado finished 121 points. Being top heavy is fine if the rest of the
roster still shows up. Nashville and Winnipeg are on this list too and they were
top heavy and bad, so the share on its own tells you nothing.

What Detroit has is a level problem, not a shape problem. The 4th leading goal
scorer was Patrick Kane at 16 and the 5th was van Riemsdyk at 15, and after that
it's 13, 11, 10, 9, 9, 9, 7 and you're into single digits fast. 3 guys hit 20
goals when the league average is 4.1, and 5 hit 15 when the average is 6.4. Add
it up and the depth is about **23 goals** short of what an average team gets from
everybody outside its top 3.

I should be fair about where those 23 come from, because it isn't all
concentration. Detroit's skater total of 239 is already about 14 below the
league's 253, and the top 3 being 9 above average accounts for most of the rest.
So "4th fewest from the depth" is partly just "Detroit doesn't score much."

## And this is where it gets annoying

Kane's in Chicago now. Van Riemsdyk isn't on the roster either. That's the 4th
and 5th leading goal scorers, 31 goals, gone.

What came back in is Viktor Arvidsson, who scored 25 in 69 games for Boston last
season, plus Keegan Kolesar, who had 6 in 82 for Vegas. 31 out, 31 in.

And here's the thing that made me laugh in a not very funny way. 25 goals would
tie Arvidsson with Raymond for 3rd on this team. The guy brought in to be depth
scoring is, on last year's numbers, a top 3 scorer here. That's how thin the
middle of this roster is.

I'd love to talk myself into him being more than that. He's a real player and 25
goals is 25 goals. He's also 33, he had 15 in 67 the year before that and 6 in 18
the year before that, and his last 4 seasons are 77, 18, 67 and 69 games. He's
never played a full 82 in his career. Betting on 25 again is betting on his best
season in 3 years repeating a year older.

## Where the goals would actually come from

The honest answer is the kids, and it's not a satisfying answer in August. Emmitt
Finnie put up 13 goals in 82 games at 21. Marco Kasper had 9 in 81 at 22. Nate
Danielson got 28 games. If Finnie goes 13 to 22 and Kasper goes 9 to 18, that's
18 goals out of nowhere and most of the depth gap closes without anybody signing
anything. If they stay flat, it doesn't, and nobody left on the free agent board
fixes 139.

## The part that argues the other way, and it's a good argument

If you're about to tell me that trading Larkin obviously makes the offense worse,
you're right, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. 34 goals is 34 goals. Move
him and the top 3 drops from 100 to somewhere around 91 even with Arvidsson
sliding up, and Detroit goes from 3 twenty-goal scorers to 2 when the league
average is 4.1. Total goals is what actually tracks points, and across the league
last season the top-3 bucket tracked the standings a bit better than the depth
bucket did. So no, this isn't "Larkin doesn't matter."

What I'd say instead is that the depth number doesn't move either way. Keep him
and you're a 92 point team with 139 from the bottom of the roster. Trade him for
a package and you're a worse team next April with 139 from the bottom of the
roster, plus whatever the package turns into in 3 years. The thing that has to
get fixed is the same thing in both worlds, and it's the one nobody's arguing
about.

Detroit finished 92 points on a minus 17 goal differential. Fixing minus 17 means
scoring more or allowing less, and if it's the scoring, it has to come from the
26 players who managed 139, because the 3 who managed 100 are already doing their
part.

Anyway that's what I've got. Am I wrong that the depth is the story? I keep
seeing people say the offense needs a top 6 forward, and I sort of think it needs
a 3rd line that can score, which is a much less fun thing to ask for. If you want
me to run the same split for another team or another season, say so and I'll do
it.
