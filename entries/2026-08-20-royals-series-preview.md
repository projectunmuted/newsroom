Detroit has won 6 of the 10 and outscored Kansas City 36 to 35 across the whole
season series. That's 3.6 runs a game against 3.5, in a league averaging 8.95
runs between the 2 sides. These games have run about 2 runs below the ordinary
baseball game and every one of them has come down to an inning.

## Before anybody gets attached to it

The number is real. It's still a maximum, and a maximum picked out of 391
pairings is going to sit in the tail whether or not anything caused it.

So here's the test. Take the league's own one-run rate this season, 520 games of
1,909, or 27.2 percent. Give every pairing exactly the number of meetings it
has actually played, so the shape of the schedule is untouched, and decide each
game with a weighted coin. Do that 20,000 times and ask how tight the tightest
pairing in each made-up league was.

**A leader reaching 8 turns up in 13 percent of those seasons**, and the average
league-leading count is 6.6. So this is a tail draw and not a miracle, which is
the same correction any "most in baseball" number needs before it gets used.

What makes it worth writing anyway is the history. The balanced schedule arrived
in 2023 and cut division pairings from 19 meetings to 13, so only the last 3
seasons compare:

| Season | Tightest pairing in baseball |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 7 of 13, Boston and Toronto |
| 2024 | 7 of 13, Pittsburgh and St. Louis |
| 2025 | 7 of 14, Cleveland and Minnesota |
| 2026, through Aug 20 | **8 of 10, Detroit and Kansas City** |

Nobody has finished a full season above 7 since the format changed. Detroit and
Kansas City are at 8 with 3 games left to play, all of them this weekend.

## The objection to my own hook, and it matters this weekend

All 7 of the Comerica games were decided by 1 run. Only 1 of the 3 at Kauffman
was. This series is at Kauffman.

That is 3 games and I'm not going to pretend it's a park effect. But it points
at the actual mechanism, which is not mystical. Kansas City is a completely
different offense at home:

| Kansas City | Record | Runs scored per game | Runs allowed per game |
|---|---|---|---|
| At Kauffman | 32-30 | 4.82 | 4.89 |
| On the road | 22-44 | 3.58 | 5.09 |

Their pitching travels. Their bats do not. A club that scores 3.58 a game away
from home plays a lot of 2-1 losses, and 7 of the 8 one-run games in this series
happened when the Royals were the visitors.

## What Detroit is, under the record

Detroit is 61-66 and 1.5 games out of the last wild card, which tells you more
about the American League than about Detroit. Their run differential is plus 82.
The 4th best team ERA in baseball at 3.57. They have scored 82 more runs than
they've allowed and they are 5 games under .500.

Here is where it goes:

| | Detroit |
|---|---|
| Games decided by 1 run | **12-22**, 29th of 30 |
| Games decided by 2 or 3 runs | 18-27 |
| Games decided by 4 or more | 30-17 |
| Saves | **26 of 54 chances**, worst conversion in baseball |
| Blown saves | **28** |
| Team ERA | 3.57, 4th |

Wednesday at PNC Park was the 28th. Detroit led 3-2 into the bottom of the
ninth, Kenley Jansen came in, and 2 solo home runs later it was over. That is
not a slump. 26 for 54 is the entire season.

And no, Detroit is not a bad road team in any way the numbers support. They
score 4.59 and allow 3.92 away from Comerica, which is better than their home
split. They're 29-35 on the road anyway, because that's what happens when the
ninth inning keeps ending badly.

## The pitching, and Riley Greene is eligible Saturday

Kansas City hasn't named a starter for any of the 3. Detroit's are set:

| | GS | IP | ERA | WHIP | K | BB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Troy Melton, Friday | 14 | 84.1 | **1.71** | 0.97 | 73 | 25 |
| Drew Anderson, Saturday | 5 | 76.1 | 4.01 | 1.30 | 87 | 29 |
| Framber Valdez, Sunday | 25 | 138.2 | 4.35 | 1.40 | 111 | 54 |

Melton's 1.71 over 84.1 innings is the best thing on either roster and it isn't
close. Kansas City's staff carries a 4.79 ERA, 28th in baseball.

The lineup news is the one fact that could change this series. Riley Greene went
on the 10-day injured list on August 12 with a right hamstring strain, which
makes **August 22 the first day he can be activated**, and that's Saturday. He
has not been activated as of Thursday morning and eligible is not the same as
back, so treat this as a thing to watch rather than a thing to count on.
Detroit is still without Kerry Carpenter, Matt Vierling, James Outman, Parker Meadows and
Wenceel Pérez, so the corners have been rookies and an infielder, and Greene's
.816 OPS over 492 plate appearances is most of what this lineup is missing.

Kansas City got Vinnie Pasquantino and Maikel Garcia back off the injured list on
Monday, designated Starling Marte for assignment the same day and released him
Wednesday.

## The call

**Detroit takes 2 of 3.** The gap between these clubs is a run and a quarter a
game of run prevention, 3.57 against 4.79, and Melton on Friday. Kansas City
gets the version of its offense that actually hits, because they're home, and
that still leaves them 28th in team ERA with 3 starters unnamed. Over 3 games
I'd rather own the better staff.

What scares me is everything in the middle of this piece. This is the tightest
matchup in baseball, Detroit loses one-run games at a rate 29th of 30, and their
closer has now blown 28. If all 3 of these come down to the ninth I have picked
the club that has been handing that inning back since April.
