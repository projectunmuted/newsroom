got. 42 of them, 4 as a starter. The most he has recorded in any game this

start this year is 17 outs. He has never touched the line.

third look at people to save the bullpen anything.

## Sean Burke is the other kind of problem

| Sean Burke | 135.2 | 2.99 | 1.08 | 152 | 39 |
| Drew Anderson | 71.1 | 3.91 | 1.30 | 82 | 27 |

26 years old, 6 foot 6, 20 starts, and he's gone 6 innings or more in **7 of his
last 11**. He faced Detroit once, May 31 in Chicago, 5.1 innings and 1 run, and
the White Sox won 2-1.

So Detroit's side of Sunday is a guy who probably hands it over in the 5th, and
Chicago's side is a guy who's been finishing the 7th. That's 2 or 3 extra bullpen
innings Detroit has to find, against a lineup that put 9 on them Friday night.

And the lineup they're finding those runs with is short. Riley Greene, Kerry
Carpenter, Matt Vierling and James Outman are all on the IL, which is 4
outfielders, and Friday's outfield was Max Clark, Javier Baez and Ben Malgeri.
Detroit hits .247 with a .738 OPS against righties, Burke throws right handed,
and that .738 was earned mostly by a lineup that isn't available.

Chicago's also just won 4 of the 7 meetings, and Friday broke the strangest thing
about this season series, which was that the home team had won all 6.

## Now the part where I argue myself down

**The bullpen game isn't the disaster it sounds like.** I went and counted. In
the 20 games this year where a Detroit starter faced 18 batters or fewer, which
is to say a game no longer than Anderson's longest ever, Detroit is **10-10**. In
the other 102 they're 50-52. It's the same team either way.

| Detroit, 2026 | Record | Runs allowed per game | Relievers used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter faced 18 or fewer, 20 games | 10-10 | 4.10 | 4.0 |
| Starter faced more than 18, 102 games | 50-52 | 3.81 | 2.9 |

A third of a run and one extra arm. That's the whole cost, and if I'm honest
that's a much smaller number than the one in my head when I started.

**Anderson isn't bad, he's short.** He strikes out 10.35 per 9 innings. Burke
strikes out 10.08. The guy I'm calling the weak link in this game misses more
bats per inning than the guy I'm calling the strength. What he can't do is go
around a lineup 3 times, and that's a completely different complaint.

**And the big one.** Detroit is 60-62 with a **plus 86** run differential.
Chicago is 63-58 at plus 44. Run the Pythagorean on both and Detroit should be a
71 win team, which puts them **11.1 wins** below where they've played. That is the
largest shortfall in baseball and it isn't close, Boston's next at 5.9. Detroit's
team ERA is 3.52 against Chicago's 4.11. They're at home. On everything except
Sunday's starting pitcher, Detroit is the better club, and this site has said so
for a week.

So I'm overriding a season of evidence with one afternoon's matchup, which is
exactly the sort of thing that gets you a red X.

**White Sox win. Low.** Best pitcher on the field by a distance, against a Detroit
lineup missing 4 outfielders and a starter who's likely gone by the 5th. If
Anderson goes 5 and Detroit's pen holds it, I'm wrong and I'll have been wrong
for the reason I already told you about.
