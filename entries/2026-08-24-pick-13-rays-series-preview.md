Comerica Park. Tonight: Rays win. The series: Tampa Bay takes 2 of 3.**

Both of these teams have played 130 games. Both have scored **587 runs.** Not
about the same, the same, to the run.

Detroit has allowed **514.** Tampa Bay has allowed **543.** So the Tigers have
the better run differential of the 2 clubs, by 29 runs, and the better team ERA,
3.62 against 3.83.

Tampa Bay is **77-53.** Detroit is **61-69.** That is a 16-game gap between 2
teams whose seasons, added up, look nearly identical.

## They are the 2 ends of the same distribution

produced, and subtract. Detroit is **11.9 wins short** of its run differential,
the largest deficit in baseball. Tampa Bay is **7.4 wins over**, the largest
surplus in baseball. Nobody else is within 5 of either one.

## The mechanism is not mysterious

Split every game by how close it was:

| Margin | Tigers | Rays |
|---|---|---|
| 1 run | **12-22** | **18-13** |
| 2 to 4 runs | 27-36 | **42-19** |
| 5 or more | **22-11** | 17-21 |

Detroit has a winning record in exactly one bucket, and it is the bucket that
decides nothing. The Tigers are the better blowout team here and the worse
close-game team by a distance. Tampa Bay is the reverse, and the reverse is
where the standings live.

The bullpens say the same thing another way:

Detroit's staff is better at not allowing runs and worse at not allowing them at
the moment it matters. 28 blown saves is the most in baseball, and it has been
this team's whole season.

gap built over 130 games does not tell you who wins on a Monday. What it does
tell you is which way a close game tends to break, and 2 of these 3 will
probably be close.
