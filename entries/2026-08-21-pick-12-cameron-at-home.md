**Tigers at Royals, Friday 8:10pm ET at Kauffman Stadium. The call: Tigers win.
Confidence Low.**

Noah Cameron threw 7 shutout innings against Detroit a month ago and gave up 2
hits doing it. That's the game everybody's going to remember walking into
Friday, and it happened at Comerica, which is the only reason I'm still picking
Detroit.

## The gap

Cameron's season ERA is 4.16. Split it by where he pitched and it stops being
one number.

| | IP | ERA | WHIP | Opponent average | Walks per batter faced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| At Kauffman | 62.2 | **5.17** | 1.55 | .287 | 25 in 282 |
| On the road | 75.2 | **3.33** | 0.95 | .196 | 18 in 298 |

That's a run and 8 tenths, and it goes the wrong way. Kauffman is a big park
with a big outfield and pitchers are supposed to like it. The league as a whole
does: run every starter with 100 innings and 15 starts through the same split
and the median guy is about 3 tenths of a run **better** at home.

Cameron is 4th of 97 in the other direction.

## Now the part that argues against me

I want this to be a bigger deal than it is, so here's the deflation.

20 of those 97 starters are more than a full run worse at home. 26 are more than
a full run worse on the road. The whole distribution has a standard deviation of
1.45, which is enormous, and that's the tell: 60-odd innings on each side is not
enough innings to tell a real venue effect from a couple of bad afternoons.
Cameron's worst home start is 7 earned in 5 against San Diego on July 19 and his
second worst is 6 in 3 and 2 thirds against Tampa Bay. Pull those two and most of
the gap goes with them.

So I'm not claiming Kauffman breaks him. I'm claiming the version of Cameron who
shut Detroit out was the road version, and Friday he's the other one, and if you
want to call that a tiebreaker rather than a thesis I won't argue.

One more thing about that July 24 game that nobody mentions: it wasn't a start.
Kansas City used an opener and Cameron came in behind him for 7 innings. It's in
his road split and not in his start log, which is correct in both places. He also
lost. Detroit won 2-1.
