**The call: Detroit takes the opener Friday night at Comerica. Confidence:
low.**

First place is coming to town, which is a sentence I did not expect to write
about the White Sox in August. They're 62-58 and they lead the AL Central.
Detroit's 60-61 and 2.5 back, holding the last wild card by half a game with 6
teams inside 2 of it. So this is a big series, and the strange thing about
Friday is that neither team is really going to start a pitcher.

## Sean Newcomb has made 1 start this year and it was against Detroit

Chicago's giving the ball to Newcomb, who's appeared in 44 games and started
exactly 1 of them. 64.1 innings across 44 outings, which is under an inning and a
half a night. His longest appearance of the whole season is 3 innings.

That 1 start? June 20, at Comerica, against these guys. He went 3 innings, faced
9 hitters, and retired all 9. Then the bullpen took it the rest of the way and
Detroit won 4-1 anyway.

So he's an opener, and everybody knows he's an opener, and Chicago isn't
pretending otherwise. To be fair to him he's been very good in the role, 2.66
ERA and a 1.13 WHIP, and he strikes out 8.4 per 9. He's just not going to be out
there in the 5th.

## This is how the whole team is built, and I don't think it's a weakness

Here's the part I went in expecting to be a punchline and came out respecting.
Chicago's bullpen has thrown **548.2 of the team's 1,071.1 innings**. That's
.512, more than half, and it's the most in baseball. Only Washington is also
over half, at .509. The median club is at .418.

Look at the last 2 columns for Chicago. Rotation 4.36, bullpen 3.85. Their
relievers are the better unit and it isn't close, so leaning on them isn't a
team falling apart, it's a team playing to the thing it's actually got. Their
pen has the 9th best ERA in the sport. Their rotation is 19th.

And it's worth saying they're in first place doing it, so whatever I think of
it, it's working.

## Detroit has the best rotation in baseball and Friday they don't have it

Detroit's rotation ERA is **3.30**, the lowest of all 30 teams. Ahead of the
Yankees, ahead of Tampa Bay, ahead of the Dodgers. That's the team's whole
identity this year, and it's the reason a 60-61 club has given up only 462 runs.

Jackson Jobe gets the ball Friday, and Jobe has thrown 5 innings this season.
All 5 came last Saturday in San Francisco, 5 shutout innings, 4 strikeouts,
16 hitters faced, and that's his entire 2026. He made 10 starts in 2025 and then
that was the last anybody saw of him until last Saturday. Nobody's letting him
see a lineup a 3rd time on Friday either.

So both dugouts are going to be into their bullpen somewhere around the 5th, and
that's what this game actually is. Detroit's pen has the 6th best ERA in baseball
at 3.74 and Chicago's is 9th at 3.85, which is basically a coin flip on quality.
The difference is that Chicago's relievers do this every night and know it, and
Detroit's are being asked to cover for a starter who isn't stretched out yet.

## Why I like Detroit anyway

The season series is 3-3 and it's the tidiest 3-3 you'll ever see. Chicago swept
them in Chicago in May. Detroit swept them at Comerica in June. All 6 games went
to the home team, and 4 of the 6 were decided by 1 run.

The home thing isn't just those 6 games either. Chicago is **37-24 at home and
25-34 on the road**. Detroit's 32-28 at Comerica. Friday is at Comerica.

And the shape of the 2 teams right now is not the shape the standings suggest.
Since June 1 Detroit is 38-23 and has outscored people by 129 runs. Chicago is
30-31 in that time at plus 32. Detroit's won 7 of 10, Chicago's lost 6 of 10.
Chicago's lead is mostly a 32-27 start that Detroit spent the summer eating into
from 22-38.

## What scares me, and it's the same thing every time

Detroit is **12-20 in 1 run games**, 4th worst in baseball. Chicago is 21-19.

That matters more than usual here, because a game where both teams empty the pen
from the 5th inning on is exactly the kind of game that ends 4-3, and 4-3 is the
score Detroit keeps losing.

The thing I can't talk myself out of is this. The nice version of Detroit's
season is the comeback: 22-38 at the end of May with a run differential of minus
39, and now they're in a playoff spot. That's all true and it's great. But run
their record against their own run differential and they were 3.6 wins short of
it through May, and they're **5.5 wins short of it since June 1**. The leak
hasn't closed while they've been climbing. It's gotten bigger. They've just been
outscoring people by so much that it stopped showing up in the standings.

Friday is a game with almost no margin in it for a team that's been losing the
close ones.

Then there's the outfield, which is now 4 deep on the injured list. Riley Greene,
Matt Vierling and Kerry Carpenter are all on the 10 day, and James Outman went on
the 7 day Thursday with a concussion. Corey Julks and Trei Cruz got called up.
Thursday's outfield was Max Clark in center, Javier Baez in left and Ben Malgeri
in right, and Malgeri and Clark are both rookies. That's a lot of new faces
against a first place team.

**Tigers win. Low.** Home team, better run differential, hotter, and I don't
love saying it out loud but I think Detroit's bullpen holds one more than
Chicago's does.

What are you watching for here? I'll be watching how long Jobe goes, because if
he's out after 4 this turns into a bullpen game Detroit didn't plan on.
