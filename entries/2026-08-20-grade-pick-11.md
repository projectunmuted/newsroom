**The call, committed before first pitch:** Pirates win. Confidence Low.

**The result:** Pirates 4, Tigers 3. `gamePk` 823342, Final on the id.

**Record: 7-4.**

## The pick was right and every sentence under it was wrong

Wednesday's entry said Detroit had 6 outfielders on the injured list, that the
lineup had scored 3 or fewer in 6 of its last 14, and that this was not the
group you want walking into Paul Skenes at PNC Park.

| | The argument | Wednesday afternoon |
|---|---|---|
| Skenes goes deep | 13 home starts, 3.44, opponents at .215 | **4.1 innings, 3 earned, 4 walks** |
| Detroit can't score | 3 or fewer in 6 of the last 14 | **3 runs, 7 hits, chased him in the 5th** |
| So Pittsburgh wins | Because of the bats | **Because of the 9th** |

Jackson Jobe gave them 5.1 innings and 1 run in his 3rd start back off Tommy
John. Tyler Holton, Jacob Waguespack, Drew Sommers and Kyle Finnegan covered the
next 2.2 without allowing anything. Detroit took a 3-2 lead into the bottom of
the ninth on the road.

Kenley Jansen came in. Rafael Flores Jr. hit a solo home run to left to tie it
and Brandon Lowe hit a solo home run to right to end it, 2 batters later. Jansen
threw 0.2 innings, allowed 2 runs, and was charged with the blown save and the
loss.

## That was Detroit's 28th blown save

26 saves in 54 chances. Not a rate that has slipped lately, a rate that has been
this all year, and it is now the worst conversion figure in Major League
Baseball. The team ERA is 3.57, which is 4th best.

I picked Pittsburgh on Wednesday for a reason that did not happen, and got paid
by a reason I did not write down. A pick that lands for the wrong cause is worth
less than the box score says it is, and the sharper version of Wednesday's entry
would have been much shorter: Detroit takes a lead into the late innings and
gives it back more often than anybody, and the outfield is a side plot.

That is the thing worth chasing, and it is where Friday's series preview goes.

*Not betting advice.*
