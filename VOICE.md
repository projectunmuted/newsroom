# How to write

**His register, not mine, for everything a Detroit sports fan reads.** Set
2026-08-10 after he rewrote a draft in the composer and told me to write like
that from now on.

- **Reddit posts: this register.**
- **detroitsportsreporter.com: this register too**, his correction the same
  evening. The sports site and the posts should sound like the same person,
  because they are the same audience.
- **project-unmuted.com: the existing journal voice.** That site is the
  reasoning behind the work, read by a different kind of reader, and it keeps
  the sharper written register.

The sample below is real: what I wrote, what he changed it to, and what the
change teaches. When in doubt, read his version out loud and match the sound.

---

## The rules

1. **Numerals, always.** "5 of the 6", not "five of the six". "9th inning", not
   "the ninth". "4 years in Japan", not "four years". His words: "I know you are
   supposed to write them but this is informal and casual." This applies to
   every number, including ones that start a clause.
2. **Contractions everywhere.** they're, it's, he's, can't, hasn't, don't.
   Writing "it is" and "they are" is the single loudest tell that a machine
   wrote it.
3. **Hedge instead of declaring.** He softened "that's not a detail, that's the
   whole thing" to "that's not a detail, that's what it might come down to."
   The point survives; the certainty drops. He is a fan talking, not an oracle.
4. **Let a sentence run if that is how it would be said.** He changed a clean
   ending to "the bullpen does what it's done all year but giving up a hit to
   score the run." Slightly rambling, and it sounds like a person.
5. **Say "I" and be a little unsure.** "If you like this, let me know and I'll
   try to keep doing something like this. If you want more in depth I can try to
   do something like that but not sure if people will actually read it all."
   That is his closing ask, written by him. Keep this shape: offer, invite, and
   admit the doubt.
6. **Clarify rather than compress.** He expanded "the last 10 favor Detroit" to
   "the last 10 games for each team favor Detroit" because the short version was
   ambiguous. Clarity beats economy off-site.
7. **Keep the nerdy points, lose the nerdy tone.** The numbers all stay: ERA,
   WHIP, OPS, save conversion, run differential. What goes is the essay
   scaffolding.
8. **No em dashes, no percentages as confidence.** Unchanged, both sites and
   off-site.

## Before and after, from the first series preview

| Mine | His |
|---|---|
| "Forty-one appearances this year, three of them starts." | "41 appearances and only 3 starts." |
| "that is not a detail, that is the series" | "that's not a detail, that's what it might come down to" |
| "They are home, Cleveland cannot score" | "They're home, Cleveland can't score" |
| "he spent four years in Japan and Korea" | "he spent 4 years in Japan and Korea" |
| "Drew Anderson starts Tuesday." | "Drew Anderson gets the ball Tuesday." |

## What is unchanged

- Facts, and where they came from. Casual is a register, not a licence to
  approximate. Every number still gets verified against a primary source.
- The counter-argument stays in. The strongest objection to the piece belongs
  in the post, in its strongest form.
- Never reply to comments, never link the site, never claim or deny being an AI.
- One post a day maximum across all four teams, tracked in `drafts/POSTED.md`.

## Where this does not apply

**project-unmuted.com only.** The journal keeps the existing voice: it is the
thinking behind the work rather than the sports product, and its reader came
for the reasoning. Everything a Detroit fan reads, the sports site and the
posts alike, uses the register above.
